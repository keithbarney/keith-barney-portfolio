Work History

Atlas Design System

Role
Design Systems Lead
Organization
Experian Consumer Services, North America
Platforms
Web, iOS, and Android

Context

I joined Experian as a product designer when the team did not have a design system. There was an existing style guide, but each domain was working in its own box and adapting it to the needs of individual projects. By the time I joined, typography, patterns, and visual aesthetics had already diverged across web, iOS, and Android. Changes did not move through the organization in a consistent or efficient way, and that lack of alignment was the issue I set out to address.

Approach

Inventory and alignment

I built buy-in with leadership around creating a design system, using the existing style guide as a starting point. I began with an inventory: collecting the materials already in use, centralizing components and typography, and turning them into a shareable library. From there, I worked with designers from the different domains to build consensus around the individual pieces. The work was as much about creating a shared way of working as it was about creating a shared visual language.

Shared team model

Once the design team was bought in, I brought development leadership and individual engineers into the process. We set up recurring conversations across design and development, formed a core team, and created Slack channels to support the people using the system. That group became the formal design systems team, and my role shifted to Design Systems Lead.

Implementation quality

Atlas continues to evolve through token work and by bringing platform-specific Figma components into a more unified system. I also championed a Design QA feedback loop to catch inaccurate visual execution, incorrect tokens, missing responsive behavior, and other deviations before release.

Adoption

Design adopted quickly

Designers welcomed a shared system and adopted Atlas relatively quickly. Reusable foundations and components reduced repeated decisions while giving teams a more consistent way to build across platforms.

Building engineering ownership

Engineering adoption took more work. Development leadership initially questioned the need for a dedicated design-system team and raised governance concerns. I made the case for Atlas and helped establish a durable cross-functional team with design and engineering contributors across platforms. Accessible behavior was built into Atlas components by default, giving teams a practical adoption path and meeting an important business requirement.

The cost of delayed adoption

Product teams naturally prioritized customer-facing and revenue-generating work over migration and technical-debt tickets, so adoption remained uneven. Updating legacy experiences required product managers to prioritize migration. When leadership later pushed to modernize Experian’s interface, teams already using Atlas could respond quickly; teams on disparate components faced slower, more painful manual updates. The lesson was strategic: broad adoption creates a foundation for future system-wide changes, from visual refreshes and marketing updates to dark mode.

Outcomes

Consistent experiences

Atlas unified typography, spacing, color patterns, design tokens, and atomic components across domains, platforms, pages, features, and customer touchpoints. Teams could work from a shared visual language instead of independently diverging from it.

Faster delivery

Teams could bring complete, reusable components into new work rather than redesigning and re-explaining the same patterns. Accessibility, responsive behavior, cross-platform support, interaction states, tokens, and other implementation requirements were already considered, reducing repetitive decisions and handoff effort.

Now

Scaling adoption

Current work is focused on the remaining Atlas migration and tech-debt gap. Leadership is now involved and has made broader adoption an action item for product teams. Progress is underway; closing that gap will make future system-wide changes faster and more consistent.

Codifying guidance

I’m creating Markdown guidance files for Atlas components, covering usage, do and don’t scenarios, and implementation considerations. The guidance also extends to broader patterns—spacing, layout, forms, and feedback—so system knowledge becomes durable, reusable support for teams.

Enabling AI-assisted design

A forward-looking goal is to give AI tools Atlas’s documented guidance, patterns, and production component code. A product brief describing a real user problem could then become input for experiences generated within those constraints—aligned to Atlas rules and actual components. This is an active direction, not a completed capability.
